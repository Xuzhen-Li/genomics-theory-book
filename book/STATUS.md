# Genomics Theory Book · Status of each theory

English | [中文](STATUS.zh.md)

Labels: **Verified** = core predictions hold repeatedly across data types; **Partly verified** = qualitative predictions hold but the quantitative part or the scope is still disputed; **Contested** = evidence conflicts; **Rejected** = core predictions have been refuted by data. The reasons and sources for each label are in the chapter.

| Ch. | Theory | Status | Reason in one line | References | Figures (published / own) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | [Mendelian inheritance and its exceptions](chapters/01-mendelian-inheritance.md) | **Verified** | All seven of Mendel’s pea characters map to cloned genes; exceptions (linkage, drive, imprinting, paramutation, organelles, polysomy) have known causes. | 36 | 1 / 2 |
| 02 | [The Hardy–Weinberg principle](chapters/02-hardy-weinberg.md) | **Verified (null model)** | The derivation is exact; departures are expected and diagnose selfing, structure, clonality, selection or genotyping error. | 29 | 1 / 2 |
| 03 | [The neutral theory of molecular evolution](chapters/03-neutral-theory.md) | **Partly verified** | Constraint and the rarity of advantageous mutations hold; the share of adaptive substitution varies widely among species and is disputed. | 29 | 1 / 2 |
| 04 | [The nearly neutral theory](chapters/04-nearly-neutral-theory.md) | **Partly verified** | Selection efficacy rises with Ne across mammals, animals, plants and mating systems; quantitative predictions depend on the DFE shape. | 31 | 1 / 1 |
| 05 | [The coalescent](chapters/05-coalescent.md) | **Verified (mathematical framework)** | Exact large-population limit of standard models, confirmed by simulation; structure, linked selection, skewed offspring and clonality make inferred histories model-dependent. | 27 | 1 / 2 |
| 06 | [The molecular clock](chapters/06-molecular-clock.md) | **Partly verified** | Divergence grows with time and relaxed-clock dating works; the strict clock fails, with rates varying by lineage, life history and timescale. | 31 | 1 / 1 |
| 07 | [Recombination landscapes and linkage disequilibrium](chapters/07-recombination-ld.md) | **Verified** | LD decays with 4Nc as predicted; LD maps agree with pedigree maps at broad scales; hotspots (PRDM9 in mammals, promoters in plants) are established. | 29 | 1 / 1 |
| 08 | [Linked selection and Lewontin’s paradox](chapters/08-linked-selection.md) | **Verified (phenomenon); Contested (relative roles, Lewontin’s paradox)** | Diversity tracks recombination in many taxa and BGS models fit human data; the share of sweeps and whether linked selection explains Lewontin’s paradox are disputed. | 27 | 1 / 1 |
| 09 | [Hard and soft sweeps](chapters/09-hard-soft-sweeps.md) | **Verified (sweeps occur); Contested (hard versus soft frequency)** | Sweep footprints are confirmed at loci with functional evidence such as LCT and tb1; whether soft sweeps dominate adaptation depends on demographic models and is disputed. | 29 | 1 / 1 |
| 10 | [Genetic load and the cost of domestication](chapters/10-genetic-load-domestication.md) | **Partly verified** | Mutation load, inbreeding depression and purging are established; a higher deleterious burden after domestication is found in rice, cassava, sunflower, maize and grapevine but not in soybean, and counts are not fitness. | 31 | 1 / 1 |
| 11 | [Muller’s ratchet and the advantages of sex](chapters/11-muller-ratchet-sex.md) | **Verified (ratchet in non-recombining genomes); Partly verified (advantages of sex)** | The ratchet is shown in experiments and neo-Y chromosomes, with faster deleterious change in many asexual lineages but not all; which advantage maintains sex in nature is unsettled. | 33 | 1 / 1 |
| 12 | [Introgression and hybrid speciation](chapters/12-introgression-hybrid-speciation.md) | **Verified (introgression); Partly verified (homoploid hybrid speciation)** | Gene flow between species and adaptive introgression are well supported; hybrid speciation without polyploidy meets strict criteria in only a few systems. | 25 | 1 / 1 |
| 13 | [Fisher’s fundamental theorem and geometric model](chapters/13-fisher-fundamental-theorem-geometric-model.md) | **Verified (fundamental theorem); Partly verified (geometric model)** | Read as Price’s exact statement about the change due to selection, the theorem holds, and additive variance in fitness is measured in wild plants and animals; the geometric model predicts fitness effects and hybrid fitness well in microbes and crosses, while its cost of complexity has weak support. | 35 | 1 / 1 |
| 14 | [Population structure: isolation by distance and shifting balance](chapters/14-population-structure.md) | **Verified (isolation by distance); Contested (shifting balance)** | Differentiation rises with distance across plants, animals and humans, with a slope that Rousset’s regression interprets; Wright’s three-phase process works in some models but has no decisive case in nature. | 34 | 1 / 1 |
| 15 | [The C-value enigma](chapters/15-c-value-enigma.md) | **Verified (genome size decoupled from gene number); Partly verified (causes)** | Genome size varies far more than gene number, mostly through repeats and polyploidy; transposon gain and DNA loss are documented, selection through cell size has correlational and some population support, and the effective population size account is not supported after phylogenetic correction. | 29 | 1 / 1 |
| 16 | [Junk DNA and the ENCODE debate](chapters/16-junk-dna-encode.md) | **Verified (large non-functional fraction); Rejected (ENCODE 80% as function)** | Comparative methods put the constrained human fraction at about 5–11% and mutational load caps it near 25%; the 80% figure measures biochemical activity, not function kept by selection. | 27 | 1 / 1 |
| 17 | [Selfish DNA and transposable elements](chapters/17-selfish-dna-transposable-elements.md) | **Verified (selfish spread); Partly verified (regular adaptive role)** | Element invasion, horizontal transfer, rarity of insertions, purging near genes and host silencing are observed directly; co-opted and large-effect adaptive insertions are known but are a small share of insertions. | 40 | 1 / 1 |
| 18 | [The drift-barrier and mutational-hazard hypotheses](chapters/18-drift-barrier-mutational-hazard.md) | **Partly verified (drift barrier); Contested (mutational hazard)** | Mutation rates fall with Ne in microbes and vertebrates, but the relation is noisy and depends on how Ne is estimated; genome expansion under drift is not supported after phylogenetic correction, and plant mitochondria with high mutation rates have huge genomes. | 31 | 1 / 1 |
| 19 | [Gene duplication: Ohno and subfunctionalization](chapters/19-gene-duplication-ohno-subfunctionalization.md) | **Verified (most duplicates lost; duplication as a source of new genes); Partly verified (subfunctionalization as the main route)** | Duplicates arise and are silenced at high rates and survivors are under purifying selection; new function, escape from adaptive conflict, complementary expression and dosage sharing are all documented, with shares that vary by organism, mode and age. | 33 | 1 / 1 |
| 20 | [The gene balance (dosage) hypothesis](chapters/20-gene-balance-dosage.md) | **Verified (dosage-balance effects and reciprocal retention); Partly verified (as a general rule for retention)** | Aneuploidy alters expression more than ploidy, and regulatory and complex genes are over-retained after whole-genome duplication and under-retained after small-scale duplication; dosage compensation and variable gene responses limit the rule. | 32 | 1 / 1 |
| 21 | [Is polyploidy an evolutionary dead end?](chapters/21-polyploidy-dead-end.md) | **Partly verified (lower diversification of recent polyploids); Contested (long-run dead end)** | Recent polyploids show lower speciation and higher extinction and few ancient duplications are retained, but the rate estimates rest on biased models and incomplete cytotype data, and many ancient duplications precede increases in diversification. | 39 | 1 / 1 |
| 22 | [Biased fractionation and subgenome dominance](chapters/22-biased-fractionation-subgenome-dominance.md) | **Verified (after many allopolyploidies); Partly verified (causes)** | Uneven gene loss and higher expression of the less fractionated subgenome are found in maize, Arabidopsis, Brassica, Mimulus, strawberry and peanut; some duplications show no bias, and transposable elements explain dominance only in part. | 27 | 1 / 1 |
| 23 | [GC-biased gene conversion](chapters/23-gc-biased-gene-conversion.md) | **Verified (mammals and birds); Partly verified (as a widespread force in eukaryotes and plants)** | GC transmission bias is measured directly in human meioses, and substitution and polymorphism patterns fit the model in mammals and birds; plant population data detect it, but tetrad sequencing in plants and fungi found no net bias. | 31 | 1 / 1 |
| 24 | [Codon usage bias](chapters/24-codon-usage-bias.md) | **Verified (selection–mutation–drift balance; translational selection in large populations); Partly verified (as the main force in plants)** | Codon preference follows expression, tRNA abundance and population size in microbes and Drosophila; between species mutation bias dominates, and in mammals and plants GC-biased gene conversion is stronger than selection. | 35 | 1 / 1 |
