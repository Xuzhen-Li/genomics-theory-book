# Mendelian segregation

```
Theory: Mendelian segregation and independent assortment
Original paper (year): Mendel G. 1866. Versuche über
  Pflanzen-Hybriden. Verhandlungen des naturforschenden
  Vereines in Brünn 4:3-47. Presented 8 February and
  8 March 1865; the volume is for 1865 and was printed
  in Brünn in 1866. No DOI. Title, journal, volume,
  pages and year checked against the Deutsches Textarchiv
  record of the 1866 printing. Distinct from the later
  historical and molecular papers cited below, which are
  tests or commentaries, not the 1866 source.
One-sentence prediction: A heterozygote transmits each
  of its two alleles to half of its gametes, unlinked
  loci assort independently, and the familiar Mendelian
  ratios (3:1, 1:2:1, 1:1, 9:3:3:1) follow in the
  appropriate crosses.
Assumptions: a sexual generation (a cross or a self);
  discrete factors; diploid meiosis; the scored character
  corresponds to a factor that can be isolated.
Which fail here: a grapevine cultivar is one seedling
  kept by cuttings, so Mendelian ratios are not seen in
  a vineyard. Clone-to-clone differences are not
  segregants. Linkage, meiotic drive, parent-of-origin
  effects, paramutation, organelle inheritance and
  polysomic segregation change the ratios, not the
  particulate gene.
Modern data / statistic: some of the seven pea characters
  Mendel scored map to specific DNA changes (Ellis et al.
  2011, DOI 10.1016/j.tplants.2011.06.006); Feng et al.
  2025 (DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-08891-6) reported the last
  three. Grapevine: Massonnet et al. 2020 (DOI
  10.1038/s41467-020-16700-z) resolved
  sex-determining-region haplotypes and a candidate
  male-sterility mutation; berry colour and seedlessness
  are used as Mendelian markers in breeding (Kobayashi
  et al. 2004, DOI 10.1126/science.1095011; Walker et al.
  2007, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-313X.2006.02997.x; Royo et al.
  2018, DOI 10.1104/pp.18.00259). Segregation
  tests must use seedling families, not a vineyard of
  cuttings.
What would falsify it: a locus whose alleles blend or
  change state in every generation without any
  identifiable genetic or epigenetic cause.
What we will not claim: that clone-to-clone differences
  in a cultivar are segregation. That Mendel 1866 has a
  DOI. That every exception abolishes particulate
  inheritance.
```
