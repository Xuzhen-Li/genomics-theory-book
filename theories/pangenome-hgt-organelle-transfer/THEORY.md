# Pangenomes, organelle-to-nucleus transfer and HGT

```
Theory: Pangenomes, organelle-to-nucleus transfer and
  horizontal gene transfer
Original paper (year): Tettelin H, Masignani V,
  Cieslewicz MJ, et al. 2005. Genome analysis of
  multiple pathogenic isolates of Streptococcus
  agalactiae: implications for the microbial
  "pan-genome". Proc Natl Acad Sci USA 102:13950-13955.
  DOI 10.1073/pnas.0506758102. That paper named the
  pan-genome (a core shared by all isolates plus a
  dispensable genome). Baumdicker, Hess and Pfaffelhuber
  2012 (DOI 10.1093/gbe/evs016) formalized gene gain and
  loss. Bayer, Golicz, Scheben et al. 2020 (DOI
  10.1038/s41477-020-0733-0) review plant pan-genomes.
  Martin et al. 2002 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.182432999) is the
  chapter's source for large-scale organelle-to-nucleus
  transfer in Arabidopsis. These are three related
  claims treated together in the chapter, not one law.
One-sentence prediction: A species is not represented
  by one genome: gene content varies among individuals
  (a pan-genome). Many nuclear genes originated in the
  bacterial ancestors of organelles. Horizontal transfer
  moves genes among distant lineages, especially in
  prokaryotes.
Assumptions: enough genomes to estimate a core and a
  variable set; organelle ancestry can be distinguished
  from later horizontal transfer; a tree is a fair
  null for gene histories.
Which fail here: in a heterozygous clonal cultivar the
  two haplotypes of one plant already differ in gene
  content, so a single reference is not the cultivar.
  Apparent transfer into the grapevine mitochondrial
  genome needs a careful filter against contamination
  and against plastid DNA that has been incorporated
  into the mitochondrion.
Modern data / statistic: Liu et al. 2024 (DOI
  10.1038/s41588-024-01967-5) built a grapevine
  pangenome and mapped loci for 29 traits; including
  structural variants raised estimated heritability.
  Zhou et al. 2019 (DOI 10.1038/s41477-019-0507-8)
  described structural variants and hemizygosity in
  grapevine. Goremykin et al. 2008 (DOI
  10.1093/molbev/msn226): the grapevine mitochondrial
  genome has incorporated a large fraction of the
  chloroplast genome; the only evidence for horizontal
  transfer into it involves fragments of two
  closteroviruses.
What would falsify it: additional grapevine genomes add
  no gene-content or structural variation beyond one
  reference, or organelle-to-nucleus and horizontal
  transfers cited in the chapter are artefacts once
  contamination and plastid insertions are removed.
What we will not claim: that rampant horizontal transfer
  shaped the grapevine mitochondrial genome (Goremykin
  et al. 2008 argued against that). That a pangenome
  estimate from cultivars is a wild-species pan-genome.
  That Tettelin 2005 is a plant paper.
```
