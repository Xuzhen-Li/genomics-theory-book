# Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium

```
Theory: Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium
Original paper (year): Hardy GH. 1908. Mendelian
  Proportions in a Mixed Population. Science 28:49-50.
  DOI 10.1126/science.28.706.49. Independently,
  Weinberg W. 1908. Über den Nachweis der Vererbung
  beim Menschen. Jahreshefte des Vereins für
  vaterländische Naturkunde in Württemberg 64:369-382.
  No DOI. Title, journal, volume and pages 369-382
  checked against the Wellcome Collection record of the
  1908 separat printing (physical description pages
  [369]-382) and against later library and biographical
  records. Stern 1943 (DOI 10.1126/science.97.2510.137)
  drew attention to Weinberg's paper; Crow 1988
  (DOI 10.1093/genetics/119.3.473) traces the early
  history.
One-sentence prediction: Under random mating in a large
  population with no selection, mutation or migration,
  genotype frequencies at an autosomal locus reach p²,
  2pq and q² after one generation and stay there.
Assumptions: a sexual generation with random mating;
  a large population; an autosomal locus; independent
  individuals, not clonal copies of one plant.
Which fail here: cuttings freeze genotypes, so
  heterozygosity can exceed 2pq. A germplasm collection
  holds clones and synonyms, and a single cultivar is
  one diploid genotype. Structure (the Wahlund effect)
  and relatedness also cause departures.
Modern data / statistic: exact tests of proportions
  (Wigginton, Cutler and Abecasis 2005, DOI
  10.1086/429864); theory for clonal and partly clonal
  diploids (Balloux, Lehmann and de Meeûs 2003, DOI
  10.1093/genetics/164.4.1635). Grapevine: Myles et al.
  2011 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1009363108) reported high
  diversity and a complex pedigree network in cultivated
  and wild samples. Unrelated cultivars should be pooled
  only after clones and synonyms are removed, and the
  sample treated as structured.
What would falsify it: after clones and structure are
  removed, genotype frequencies in a randomly mating
  sexual sample systematically depart from p², 2pq and
  q² with no selection, mutation, migration or
  genotyping error to explain the departure.
What we will not claim: that a cultivar collection is a
  random-mating population. That Weinberg 1908 has a
  DOI. That a heterozygote excess in clones rejects the
  principle, rather than its random-mating assumption.
```
