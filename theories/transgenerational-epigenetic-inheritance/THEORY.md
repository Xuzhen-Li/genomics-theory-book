# Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance

```
Theory: Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance
Original paper (year): Jablonka E, Raz G. 2009.
  Transgenerational Epigenetic Inheritance: Prevalence,
  Mechanisms, and Implications for the Study of Heredity
  and Evolution. Q Rev Biol 84:131-176. DOI
  10.1086/598822. That paper is a review that stated the
  claim as a general phenomenon; it is the framing
  source used in the chapter, not a single experimental
  test. Cubas, Vincent and Coen 1999 (DOI 10.1038/43657)
  is an early molecular case (a heritable epimutation at
  Lcyc in Linaria). Chandler 2007 (DOI
  10.1016/j.cell.2007.02.007) reviews paramutation.
  Johannes et al. 2009 (DOI 10.1371/journal.pgen.1000530)
  is a later experimental assessment of epigenetic
  variation and complex traits. Distinct from the
  Rodin-Riggs 2003 epigenetic-complementation note.
One-sentence prediction: Epigenetic states (for example
  DNA methylation or RNA-mediated silencing) can be
  transmitted across generations through the germline
  and can contribute to heritable phenotypic variation.
Assumptions: transmission is through the germline, not
  only mitosis; resetting at meiosis and seed development
  is incomplete; the state is epigenetic rather than a
  hidden DNA change.
Which fail here: cuttings and grafts bypass meiosis and
  seed development, where much resetting happens.
  Methylation that persists among grapevine clones is
  mitotic inheritance; it is not transgenerational
  inheritance through the germline.
Modern data / statistic: Schellenbaum et al. 2008 (DOI
  10.1186/1471-2229-8-78) found DNA methylation
  polymorphisms in grapevine somaclones. Cochetel et al.
  2025 (DOI 10.1186/s13059-025-03858-2) reported that
  methylation marks are inherited across clones of a
  historical hybrid, though less conserved than genetic
  polymorphisms. Callipo et al. 2026 (DOI
  10.1186/s13059-026-04184-x) found that CG and CHG
  methylation can reconstruct clonal relationships in
  Pinot noir. These are tests of mitotic persistence,
  not of germline transgenerational inheritance.
What would falsify it: in a sexual lineage, a candidate
  epigenetic state is not transmitted through the
  germline once DNA sequence change has been excluded,
  or it is reset every generation.
What we will not claim: that clonal methylation in
  grapevine is transgenerational inheritance through the
  germline. That Jablonka and Raz 2009 is a primary
  experiment. That every tissue-culture methylation
  change is stably inherited through seed.
```
