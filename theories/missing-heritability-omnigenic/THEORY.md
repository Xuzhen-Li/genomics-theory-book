# Missing heritability and the omnigenic model

```
Theory: Missing heritability and the omnigenic model
Original paper (year): Fisher RA. 1919. The correlation
  between relatives on the supposition of Mendelian
  inheritance. Trans R Soc Edinb 52:399-433. DOI
  10.1017/S0080456800012163. That paper is the
  Mendelian basis for heritability of quantitative
  traits. Manolio et al. 2009 (DOI 10.1038/nature08494)
  reviewed the gap between significant association hits and
  family-based heritability, which is called missing
  heritability. The term appeared earlier in Maher 2008
  (DOI 10.1038/456018a). Boyle, Li and Pritchard 2017 (DOI
  10.1016/j.cell.2017.05.038) stated the omnigenic
  model: association signals cover most of the genome,
  and most heritability comes from genes outside core
  pathways. Visscher, Hill and Wray 2008 (DOI
  10.1038/nrg2322) is a review of heritability concepts,
  not the source of either claim.
One-sentence prediction: Most of the heritability not
  captured by significant hits is hidden in many small
  effects (and in variants that arrays miss), rather
  than absent; under the omnigenic model, most
  heritability comes from genes outside core pathways.
Assumptions: family-based heritability is a fair
  denominator, or biases in that denominator are
  measured; markers tag the causal variants; a core
  versus peripheral gene distinction can be stated.
Which fail here: a grapevine association panel is a
  structured collection of clones and pedigrees, not a
  human GWAS cohort. Family-versus-SNP gaps estimated
  in humans are not automatically the same gap in a
  clonal crop.
Modern data / statistic: Flutre et al. 2022 (DOI
  10.1093/g3journal/jkac103) found, in 279 grapevine
  cultivars and 127 traits, that denser markers
  increased the share of genetic variance explained by
  SNPs, and that prediction accuracy exceeded 0.42 for
  half of the response variables. Liu et al. 2024 (DOI
  10.1038/s41588-024-01967-5) reported that including
  structural variants from a grapevine pangenome raised
  estimated heritability by 22.78% on average. These
  tests support hidden heritability in untagged variants
  and in many loci; they are not a test of the
  omnigenic core-versus-peripheral claim in grapevine.
  The omnigenic model is not tested in grapevine here.
What would falsify it: after dense sequence and
  structural variants are included, and after inflation
  of family estimates is accounted for, most
  heritability still remains unexplained, or association
  signals are confined to a small set of core genes.
What we will not claim: that Flutre 2022 or Liu 2024
  tested the omnigenic model. That the 22.78% figure is
  a human missing-heritability estimate. That Fisher
  1919 stated the omnigenic model.
```
