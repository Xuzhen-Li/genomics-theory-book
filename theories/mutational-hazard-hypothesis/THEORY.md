# Mutational-hazard hypothesis

```
Theory: Mutational-hazard hypothesis
Original paper (year): Lynch M, Conery JS. 2003. The
  Origins of Genome Complexity. Science 302:1401-1404.
  DOI 10.1126/science.1089370. The chapter traces the
  named hypothesis from that argument, through Lynch
  2006 on eukaryotic gene structure (DOI
  10.1093/molbev/msj050), to Lynch, Koskella and Schaack
  2006 on organelle genomes (DOI 10.1126/science.1118884):
  where effective size is similar, genomes with lower
  mutation rates should accumulate more non-coding DNA.
  Smith 2016 (DOI 10.1111/mec.13742) is a later review
  of the organelle case, not the source. Distinct from
  the drift-barrier note (Lynch 2011, on the mutation
  rate itself) and from the Lynch-Conery 2003
  genome-complexity note, which treats the same 2003
  paper as a claim about complexity rather than as the
  mutational-hazard statement.
One-sentence prediction: Each added intron, regulatory
  site or stretch of non-coding DNA raises the rate of
  mutation to defective alleles; that disadvantage is
  weak and is overwhelmed by drift when long-term
  effective size is small. In organelles, the hazard
  scales with the mutation rate.
Assumptions: a meaningful long-term effective size; the
  added feature is a mutational target, not a selected
  function; when organelles are compared, effective size
  is similar enough that mutation rate is the main
  variable.
Which fail here: a cultivar panel is not long-term
  effective size. Plant mitochondria, including
  grapevine's, are the main counterexample and test
  case in the chapter.
Modern data / statistic: Goremykin et al. 2008 (DOI
  10.1093/molbev/msn226) describe the grapevine
  mitochondrial genome as a test case. Vondras et al.
  2019 (DOI 10.1186/s12864-019-6211-2) found heterozygous
  somatic mutations in Zinfandel clones more frequent in
  intergenic space than in exons; that is a somatic
  pattern, not a species-level test of the hazard.
What would falsify it: in a fair comparison, non-coding
  DNA does not accumulate more when long-term effective
  size is smaller, or when organelle mutation rate is
  lower, after repair and other alternatives are taken
  into account.
What we will not claim: that this note replaces the
  existing Lynch-Conery 2003 genome-complexity note.
  That Vondras 2019 estimated long-term effective size.
  That Goremykin 2008 confirmed the hazard in grapevine
  (it is cited as a test case).
```
